This project is conducted strictly for **historical preservation, educational analysis, digital software archaeology, and interoperability research** under the following statutory frameworks:

#### A. United States Copyright Law — Fair Use (17 U.S.C. § 107)
Under Section 107 of the U.S. Copyright Act, the fair use of a copyrighted work for purposes such as criticism, comment, scholarship, research, and non-commercial archival preservation is not an infringement of copyright. In evaluating fair use, the following factors apply:
1. **Purpose & Character**: The use is non-profit, educational, and transformative (updating legacy runtimes to modern .NET 4.8 and Vulkan 1.3 to prevent bit rot).
2. **Nature of the Work**: The software was originally released in 2008–2010 and has been discontinued as a standalone retail product for over a decade.
3. **Market Impact**: This preservation project creates zero negative economic impact on the market for or value of the copyrighted work, as standalone CityScape is no longer commercially sold or supported.

#### B. Reverse Engineering for Interoperability
- **United States — DMCA § 1201(f)**: Permits reverse engineering of computer programs for the sole purpose of identifying and analyzing elements necessary to achieve interoperability with independently created software.
